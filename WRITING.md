# Prose guidelines

Write as if you're explaining the project to a colleague who has work to do.

- Start with what happened or what the reader needs. Use concrete details to tell the story; don't invent a personal experience or an outcome.
- Use specific nouns and verbs. "The drill adds one Conditional Access policy" says more than "the tool empowers teams to build resilience."
- Cut sentences that only introduce, repeat or praise another sentence. Drop slogans and claims such as "seamless" or "production-ready" unless you can explain and support them.
- Link factual claims to sources you've opened and checked. Keep reported events separate from examples, assumptions and advice.
- Say what's available, what's planned and what can go wrong. Name the limits and recovery steps rather than promising safety.
- Let sentences vary in length. Contractions are fine. Don't force punchlines, dramatic fragments, neat groups of three or repeated "not X, but Y" contrasts.
- Use paragraphs for explanations, lists for steps and tables for comparisons. Don't give every thought its own heading.
- Keep each paragraph or list item on one source line. Let the editor wrap prose; preserve code, tables and diagrams as written.
- Read it aloud once. If you wouldn't say it to a colleague, rewrite it. If cutting it loses no meaning, cut it.
